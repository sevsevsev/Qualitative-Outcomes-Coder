## Q1. Anchor check (domain)

Y2 has no single anchor. `frameworkBasis` names four frameworks, one or two per code, and categories A and B are
the codebook's own groupings. That is acceptable under S1.6 because each code matches a component of its own
cited framework. The risk is the one found below: a code can drift from its framework with nothing to flag it.
Currency, per framework:

- **Hidi & Renninger (2006).** Still the standard citation. I found no revision. I did not search exhaustively
  for later Renninger & Hidi restatements. A full-text copy with the Taylor & Francis header (Educational
  Psychologist 41(2), 111-127, DOI 10.1207/s15326985ep4102_4) is at
  https://sciencecommunication.ut.ee/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/Article-5-The-Four-Phase-Model-of-Interest-Development.pdf
  (University of Tartu course copy, not the publisher's site). The registry's Swarthmore page has no full text:
  "This document is currently not available here." Confidence high.
- **Expectancy-value theory: renamed.** Eccles, J. S., & Wigfield, A. (2020), Contemporary Educational
  Psychology 61, 101859. The ACU research-bank record
  (https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/8w507/...) quotes the abstract: "justify our decision to call the
  theory Situated Expectancy-Value Theory (SEVT)." Not superseded in substance, since utility value remains a
  component. Confidence high.
- **NCAS: under review, and the coalition's name has changed.** A Young Audiences post dated Feb 19, 2025
  (https://www.youngaudiences.org/whyarts/announcing-review-national-arts-standards) says "the National
  Coalition for Arts Standards (NCAS) will launch a thorough survey to collect data on the National Standards".
  The site footer reads "© Young Audiences, Inc. 2026". A March 2026 process update
  (https://www.nationalartsstandards.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/NCAS%20Standards%20Process%20Updates%20for%20the%20Field%20-%20Final%202026.pdf)
  says the review covers "all five arts disciplines—Dance, Media Arts, Music, Theatre, and Visual Arts". No
  revised standards and no release date are announced. The 2014 standards stand. Under S1.4, keep "National
  Coalition for Core Arts Standards (2014)" and add a note on the current name and the review. Recheck when
  revised standards appear. Confidence high on the facts. I could not confirm who formally stewards the
  standards: the 2025 post calls Young Audiences one of ten members.
- **Search Institute 40 Assets.** The asset text is unchanged ("Copyright ©1997"). The page says the assets are
  "written for an optimal age range of 12-18". It is current but tier D.
- **Muhammad, Five Pursuits.** See Y2.1. The site gives no book years, which the registry already notes.

## Y2.1 Enjoyment & Joy

**Finding 1: the code redefines Muhammad's Joy while still citing it (S1.6 "not allowed").** Muhammad's Joy is
a pedagogical pursuit. The site (https://www.hillpedagogies.com/) defines it as "the teaching, uplifting, and
recognition of beauty, aesthetics, truth, benefits, and personal fulfillment within humanity and the world."
The *Unearthing Joy* blurb on the same site describes joy as "wellness, beauty, healing, and justice". Y2.1
defines a learner's affect instead: "fun, happiness, excitement, pride". The 2026-09-27 verifier already called
the fit "loose", and research-basis.md concedes it ("Muhammad's framework is a pedagogical model"). This is not
one of the four allowed deviation cases. Confidence high.

**Smallest change: re-anchor (citation fix, PATCH).** Pekrun, R. (2024), "Control-Value Theory: From Achievement
Emotion to a General Theory of Human Emotions," Educational Psychology Review 36, Article 83
(https://repository.essex.ac.uk/39185/1/Pekrun%20Ed%20Psych%20Rev%202024%20Control-Value%20Theory.pdf). It names
"concurrent activity emotions such as enjoyment or boredom during learning", and it applies across settings:
"Achievement settings in today's world, such as school, university, and the workplace". This is tier C and
cross-setting. It fits "enjoying activities" and "excitement to attend" directly. Supporting source:
Fredricks, Blumenfeld & Paris (2004), emotional engagement
(https://www.isbe.net/Documents/engagement-concept.pdf, an ISBE-hosted full text). It says "Emotional engagement
refers to students' affective reactions in the classroom ... including interest, boredom, happiness, sadness,
and anxiety". That is a classroom construct, so its fit is partial for programs. The Y1 pilot rejected
Fredricks on an abstract-only basis; this is a full text. Muhammad can stay as a pointer in `basis` ("Joy as a
pedagogical aim") but should not be the Source Framework line.

**Finding 2: "pride in being part of it" sits outside every source I found.** Pekrun's pride is an outcome
emotion ("retrospective outcome emotions like pride and shame"), meaning pride in an achievement, not in
belonging to a group. Adjudicated S008 ("proud to be part of the program", gold Y2.1, alt Y3.1) depends on this
phrase, so the wording cannot simply be dropped. Smallest change: **log an FD (codebook-defined element,
counted)**, or rely on Pekrun's pride as partial support and say so in `basis`. Confidence medium.

Real use: 19/3 unique texts ("joy in learning", "excited to come to school", "playfulness"). All of them are
affect, which supports the Pekrun framing.

## Y2.2 Curiosity & Triggered Interest

**Finding 3: the tier-B source for curiosity is already in the registry but not cited here.** Rule 14 sends
"curiosity or open-mindedness -> Y2.2" as a CASEL skill with no dedicated code. The CASEL PDF that the registry
already uses (`casel-2020`) lists "Demonstrating curiosity and open-mindedness" under Responsible
Decision-Making. Smallest change: **add `casel-2020` support for Y2.2** and its codebook name. This is a PATCH
with no FD, since it brings rule 14 and the citation into line. Confidence high. A second tier A/B source is
OECD SSES, which defines curiosity as "Interest in ideas and love of learning, understanding and intellectual
exploration; an inquisitive mind-set." (Houston city report, OECD 2021,
https://search.oecd.org/education/ceri/social-emotional-skills-study/sses-houston-report.pdf). It is
cross-national and covers ages 10 and 15.

**Finding 4: two include items go past phase 1 (unlogged departure).**
- *Asking questions.* In Hidi & Renninger, self-generated questions mark the later phases. Phase 3: "The student
  begins to regularly generate his or her own 'curiosity' questions about the content". Phase 4: "A
  well-developed individual interest may result in a student generating and seeking answers to curiosity
  questions". Phase 1 is "sparked by environmental or text features such as incongruous, surprising
  information" and is "typically, but not exclusively, externally supported". The tool cut both of these
  sentences short. Questions asked *during a demonstration* (S128) still fit phase 1, because the trigger is
  external. CASEL's curiosity bullet (Finding 3) covers the rest. With that citation added, no FD is needed for
  this item.
- *Willingness to try a new activity / an activity that made them nervous* (adjudicated S049, S080). No fetched
  source covers it: triggered interest is a response to something, not a willingness. CASEL "open-mindedness" is
  at most partial. Kashdan's "stress tolerance" curiosity dimension would be a lead, but I could not fetch it
  (the GESIS page needs JavaScript). Smallest change: **log an FD (codebook-defined element, counted)**, or have
  the verifier rate CASEL open-mindedness. Confidence medium.

Real use: 35/13 unique texts. Several are "interest in STEM/theatre" with no phase signal, which matches the
model's current default. No change suggested.

## Y2.3 Sustained & Individual Interest

**Finding 5: the citation is fine, and only the copy needs fixing.** Phases 2-4 are confirmed in the full text:
"Maintained situational interest refers to a psychological state of interest that is subsequent to a triggered
state", and "Emerging individual interest refers to a psychological state of interest as well as to the
beginning phases" (both truncated by the tool). Smallest change: **fix citation**: replace the Swarthmore URL
(which has no full text) with the full-text copy for Y2.2 and Y2.3, or the DOI plus that copy. A pointer could
be added: a young person who regularly generates their own questions about a topic belongs in Y2.3, not Y2.2
(hint, PATCH). Confidence high. No row data shows that confusion yet, so the pointer is optional.

Real use: 40/5. "Appreciation of art/music" rows (about 4) land here today. CP-09-04 in PR #62 moves them to
Y2.5, so this needs no action here.

## Y2.4 Relevance & Utility Value

**Finding 6: update the expectancy-value citation.** The 2020 restatement uses the theory's current name, and
its definition matches the code: utility value is "how well a particular task fits into an individual's present
or future plans". The four value components are "intrinsic value, attainment value, utility value, and cost".
That copy is third-party (msofc.org, https://msofc.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/2020-Situated-Expectancy-Value-Theory.pdf),
the same weakness as today's pbworks copy, and the Elsevier DOI is paywalled. Smallest change: **fix
citation**. Cite Eccles & Wigfield (2020) as the current statement and keep 2000 as the original, with a note
on the SEVT name. Hulleman (2010) stays direct. Confidence high on the rename; medium on the copy.

**Finding 7: "or community" in the definition has no source (unlogged departure).** Neither SEVT ("present or
future plans") nor Hulleman ("relevant to their lives") names community. Real rows use it ("the potential impact
STEM & CS have on their community"). Smallest change: **narrow** to "useful for one's goals, future plans or
daily life", which Hulleman covers. That is MINOR, and no design-gold row depends on "community" (S115 is
careers). Otherwise log an FD. Confidence medium. "Valuing what is learned" in the include line fits subjective
task value as a whole, so it can stay once SEVT is cited.

## Y2.5 Creative Expression & Performance (FD-P16; candidate wording from CP-09-04)

**Finding 8: FD-P16 is out of date and argues against its own case.**
- The anchor line lists only Creating and Performing/Presenting/Producing. The code (and the CP-09-04 include
  "appreciating, critiquing or connecting art to personal meaning") also covers Responding ("Understanding and
  evaluating how the arts convey meaning") and Connecting ("Relating artistic ideas and work with personal
  meaning and external context"), both confirmed on https://www.nationalartsstandards.org/.
- The case is merged-components, which S1.6 allows when "coders can't tell two framework components apart".
  The FD's evidence is "creating and presenting were never confused in the gold", and research-basis.md gives
  ease of use as the reason. That shows coders *can* tell them apart. The honest reason is that the outcome
  statements do not need the split. Under S1.6, merging only because coding gets easier is "departing … only
  because accuracy improves".
- Smallest change: **fix FD-P16** (PATCH, deviation log). List all four processes. Restate the evidence as
  "statements rarely name one process without the other" (S077 exhibits and builds pride; H008 designs and
  presents) or, if Severin prefers, change the case to codebook-defined (counted). Confidence high on the anchor
  line; medium on the case.

**Finding 9: creative writing is routed to Y2.5 but NCAS does not cover it (unlogged departure).** The Y2.5
definition names "writing", Y1.1's useInstead sends "creative writing as self-expression" here, and rule 9 says
the same. NCAS covers five disciplines only (process update above: "Dance, Media Arts, Music, Theatre, and
Visual Arts"). Real rows depend on it: "Engage reluctant writers and offer them a pathway to personal
expression", and "express their experiences and tell their story through words, drawings, and design". Smallest
change: **add a citation** rather than an FD. The OECD *Framework for the Assessment of Creative Thinking in
PISA 2021: Third Draft* (the framework for the 2022 creative thinking assessment) defines "'Creative expression'
refers to instances where creative thinking is involved in communicating one's inner world to others", with a
written-expression domain: "students will need to demonstrate a capacity to express their imagination in a
written format". This is tier A/B and cross-national. Caveat: I fetched a third-party copy
(https://teachertoolkit.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/PISA-2021-creative-thinking-framework.pdf). The
oecd.org framework chapter pages returned navigation only, so the verifier needs an OECD-hosted copy. CMEC's
highlights confirm the four domains, including "Written expression" and "Visual expression". Confidence medium
until the OECD copy is fetched.

**Finding 10: bare "creativity" lands here without a source.** About 6 real texts ("creativity", "inspire
creative thinking") were coded Y2.5. Creativity as a skill is not an NCAS process. OECD SSES defines creativity
as "Generating novel ways to do or think about things through exploring, learning from failure, insight and
vision." (Houston report, above), and PISA's definition is "the competence to engage productively in the
generation, evaluation and improvement of ideas". Smallest change: a **hint** ("creativity or creative thinking
with no product named -> Y2.5"), with SSES/PISA cited, or Y2 domain-only under CP-09-09. Severin should pick
one. Confidence low-medium, because no gold row covers this yet.

Registry hygiene: the top-level NCAS `excerpt` is "Dance • Media Arts • Music • Theatre and Visual Arts", which
supports no component. The supports excerpt joins two definitions with an ellipsis, which fails S1.5's single
passage. Replace it with one process definition per support. This is a PATCH.

## Y2.6 Participation in Enriching Activities (candidate wording from CP-09-01/02)

**Finding 11: the citation holds, and the label is stale.** The Search Institute page has the Constructive Use
of Time assets: "Young person spends three or more hours per week in sports, clubs, or organizations at school
and/or in the community", plus Creative Activities and Religious Community. These match the candidate's "outside
it (sports, arts, clubs, faith groups)". The `basis` text says "(located)" and the registry component says "(live
registry status: located)", but the status is verified. Fix both (data PATCH, as for Y1). Confidence high.

**Finding 12: optional tier-A support.** The National Survey of Children's Health (HRSA MCHB) indicator reads
"Children ages 6 to 17 who participated in organized activities outside of school, such as sports teams or
lessons" (2023-2024 average, via the KIDS COUNT Data Center,
https://datacenter.aecf.org/data/tables/11513-extracurricular-activities). Fit is partial: it covers
participation outside school only, not returning to a program. Add it as cross-sector support only (tier A
beside tier D). Confidence medium. This is nice to have, not a fix.

**Finding 13: fidelity of "returning (inside the program)".** The assets measure weekly hours, not
re-enrollment. Under the age rule this is not a population mismatch. Returning to a youth program is still
participation in a youth organization, so I rate it in scope (partial). No FD.

**Finding 14: in-session "engagement" lands here (a taxonomy gap, not an action).** Rows like "Increased
engagement in sessions" and "Increased student engagement in program activities" (about 5 texts) are coded Y2.6.
CP-09-01 notes that held-out [held-out row] (engagement in sessions) was adjudicated Y2.6. Neither Search Institute nor the
candidate definition ("choice to take part") covers engagement *quality*. Fredricks's behavioral engagement
("Behavioral engagement draws on the idea of participation", truncated) would cover it if Severin wants Y2.6 to
hold it. That would be a widening (MINOR) and should be batched with the Y1 pilot's engagement item 7 (Y1.9
vs Y2), not done here. The domain is named "Engagement" yet has no code defined as engagement. This is the most
visible structural gap. Confidence medium.

## Q4/Q5. Taxonomy and observability

- Category A follows Hidi & Renninger's phases (Y2.2, Y2.3), plus enjoyment and value, which together resemble
  SEVT's intrinsic (enjoyment) and utility values. Category B joins two unrelated constructs (arts processes and
  activity participation). It is a codebook grouping, harmless for coding because only codes are assigned.
- Missing compared with the field: a general engagement code (Finding 14) and creativity as a disposition
  (Finding 10). Both can be handled with hints or domain-only coding. Neither justifies a new code (S2.2), and
  either would cost enum budget.
- Observability is fine for all six codes. Every construct appears as written outcome verbs in the real runs.
