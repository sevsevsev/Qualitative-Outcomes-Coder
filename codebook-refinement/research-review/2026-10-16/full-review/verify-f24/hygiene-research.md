# F2.4 personal hygiene items: source research (2026-10-17)

Question: is there a published framework, indicator, survey item, statute or agency guidance that
names having personal hygiene items (soap, shampoo, toothpaste, toothbrush, deodorant; hygiene
kits) as an indicator of material well-being or deprivation, for a child, a young person or a household?

Everything here is a **lead**. The excerpts come from WebFetch page readers (a small model
summarises each page), not from raw text. An independent verifier must re-fetch each one (CLAUDE.md
hard rules). The cwpm.acf.gov host refused a direct curl through the proxy, so the CWPM excerpt was
seen only through WebFetch.

## Details

### f2hyg-01 (best candidate). JRF, destitution definition
- cite_as: Joseph Rowntree Foundation (2025). *Destitution in the UK: income thresholds for October 2025.*
- url opened: https://www.jrf.org.uk/deep-poverty-and-destitution/destitution-in-the-uk-income-thresholds-for-october-2025
- Excerpts (page reader):
  - "People are considered destitute if they have lacked 2 or more of the following essentials over the past month" (20 words)
  - "Basic toiletries (such as soap, shampoo, toothpaste, and a toothbrush)." (10 words)
  - Also on the page: "Clothing and footwear (appropriate for the weather)."
- Framing: a deprivation indicator (lacking essentials because one cannot afford them). It is measured
  for people and households, not as a child-specific item; thresholds are given for households with
  children.
- Fit: **direct** for the goods (soap, shampoo, toothpaste, toothbrush) and for the affordability
  condition already in F2.4's definition. Deodorant is not named; under G5 it reads as an instance
  of "basic toiletries" ("such as" introduces an open list). It is broader than F2.4 on population
  (people of any age), so under G3 a partial population fit does not need an FD.
- Tier: STANDARDS S1.2 tier D (a named framework from an established nonprofit), with a university
  research team (I-SPHERE) behind the measure. It is neither statutory nor an official statistic. It
  also describes severe deprivation (destitution) rather than general deprivation. The verifier and
  Severin should weigh whether a destitution measure can anchor a code that is otherwise about
  ordinary child deprivation. It is the same kind of measure, with a deeper threshold.

### f2hyg-02. I-SPHERE / Heriot-Watt, Destitution in the UK project page
- url opened: https://i-sphere.site.hw.ac.uk/research-projects/destitution-in-the-uk/
- Excerpts: "People who have lacked two or more of six essentials over the past month, because they cannot afford them." (20 words); "Basic toiletries (soap, shampoo, toothpaste, toothbrush), or" (7 words)
- The page reader says the 2023 estimate includes "around one million children" (verify).
- Fit: direct (the research team's own copy of the f2hyg-01 measure). Prefer citing the JRF report or
  page together with this one. The full 2023 report PDF on jrf.org.uk was not opened. Lead: open it for
  the methods appendix on how the toiletries item is asked and whether children are assigned household status.

### f2hyg-03. ACF Child Welfare Policy Manual 8.3B.1, Q9
- cite_as: U.S. DHHS, ACF Children's Bureau, *Child Welfare Policy Manual*, 8.3B.1 Title IV-E Foster
  Care Maintenance Payments Program, Payments, Allowable costs, Question 9 (7/6/05; 11/7/19). Legal
  reference: Social Security Act 475(4)(A) [42 U.S.C. 675(4)(A)].
- url opened: https://cwpm.acf.gov/pdfs/CW%20Policy%20Database%20-%20Section%208.3B.1%20Policy%20Questions%20&%20Answers.pdf
- Excerpt (page reader): "items related to personal hygiene; cosmetics; over-the-counter medications and special dietary foods" (13 words). Answer opens: "The Social Security Act requires that the title IV-E foster care maintenance payment is to cover the cost of" ... "personal incidentals".
- Framing: official federal guidance on a statutory duty, for a child in foster care. It is a cost the
  payment covers, not an outcome indicator.
- Fit: **partial**. Under G4 a supported statutory duty is partial support with no FD, the same
  footing the CP already gives 675(4)(A) for clothing and school supplies. It says what "personal
  incidentals" in 675(4)(A) means, so the CP's existing secondary citation of 675(4)(A) would cover
  hygiene as well once this guidance is verified. Tier A.

### f2hyg-04 / f2hyg-05. WHO/UNICEF JMP hygiene ladder; SDG 6.2.1b
- urls opened: https://washdata.org/node/24 ; https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/files/Metadata-06-02-01b.pdf
- Excerpts: "Availability of a handwashing facility with soap and water at home." (11 words); "the proportion of population with a handwashing facility with soap and water available at home." (16 words)
- Framing: a household indicator, but it measures a handwashing facility (observed by an enumerator),
  not personal hygiene goods.
- Fit: **partial at most** (soap only, at a household station). It does not support toothpaste,
  deodorant or shampoo. Use it as supporting evidence, not as an anchor.

### f2hyg-06. Global MHH Monitoring Group, Indicator 7

- Fit: **none** for F2.4. It is a school facility indicator, and the same group's Indicator 1 (menstrual

### f2hyg-08. RHYTTAC RHY Program FAQ (April 2024)
- url opened: https://www.rhyttac.net/assets/RHY%20Program%20Frequently%20Asked%20Questions_April%202024.pdf

## Judgment

- **Best candidate: JRF / I-SPHERE "Destitution in the UK" (f2hyg-01 with f2hyg-02).** It names soap,
  shampoo, toothpaste and a toothbrush as an essential whose unaffordable lack defines deprivation.
  Fit is **direct** for the items and **partial (broader)** on population. ACF CWPM Q9 (f2hyg-03) is
  official U.S. partial support. It extends the CP's existing 675(4)(A) citation to "items related
  to personal hygiene".
- **Framework fidelity:** if f2hyg-01/02 verify, F2.4 can name basic toiletries at `framework`
  fidelity. This needs JRF Destitution added to F2's Framework Basis as an "(F2.4 only)" anchor, with
  CWPM 8.3B.1 Q9 attached to the 675(4)(A) secondary item. Under G3 and G5, deodorant and "hygiene
  kits" read as instances of "basic toiletries". Two caveats for Severin: (1) JRF is tier D (nonprofit
  and academic), not official statistics, and it measures *destitution*, a deeper threshold than
  EU-SILC's child deprivation; (2) no child-specific official deprivation item list found (EU-SILC,
  UK child index, Innocenti/UNICEF items) names hygiene goods, so the child-level anchor stays EU-SILC
  for the other goods only.
- If the verifier rejects f2hyg-01/02, hygiene rests only on CWPM Q9 (partial, statutory duty, G4)
  plus JMP (partial, soap only). That probably still avoids an FD under G4 for "personal hygiene
  items" in general. It is weaker, and Severin should rule on it.
