# Review of the exhaustive-search proposals (2026-10-18)

143 proposals reviewed against the checkers' results (batches 11 to 23), the current map (L-01 to L-106) and codebook 3.3.0. Every row is in `review_decisions.csv`.

**Decisions:** 40 accept, 73 accept-with-changes, 30 reject (24 merged into another row, 6 rejected outright on evidence). **Final actions:** 7 new tested links, 29 strengthen, 17 revise, 60 new-not-recommended, 30 none. **Tiers:** 15 tested (2 strong, 3 good, 10 suggestive), 5 associated, 93 not-recommended.

**Bar used.** For tested-suggestive I required one clean randomized trial or two independent strong quasi-experiments of the same model, measured on the indicator or a state test. One quasi-experiment, or several studies by one team, stays not-recommended. Claims marked unsupported, unreachable or misattributed were dropped, with one exception: a misattributed claim was kept where the corrected citation still says what the row needs (Q-012, Q-017, Q-038, Q-049, R-024, R-036, T-023). For partial claims I used only the confirmed part and the checker's figures.

## What changed and why

**New tested links (all suggestive).** Attendance (S1): Check and Connect in grades 5 to 7 (P-05), the foster-care education specialist (P-07), SPARK tutoring (P-09), multi-summer programs (P-11), school telehealth (P-12) and Safe Passage routes (P-14). Reading (S5, S6): repeated, child-chosen free books over three or more years (PR-06), from two randomized trials on state reading tests (0.14 and about 0.10 standard deviations). A single summer of book mailing does not qualify.

**Strengthened.** L-28 (personalized absence messages to families) is confirmed as strong by the federal multi-district and 47-district trials. L-01 (reading tutoring) stays strong and now covers partner-run tutoring by paid national-service tutors; plan on 0.05 to 0.15 on the state test. L-03 (ninth- and tenth-grade math tutoring) rises to good from three trials of one model. L-83 (more days in school raise scores) rises to good.

**New or revised associated links:** L-44 (21st Century Community Learning Centers and attendance, see below), L-57 (school sports and in-season attendance), L-87 (homelessness and eviction), L-101 (middle-grades absence and dropout, adds the Chicago Longitudinal Study) and L-102.

**Do-not-claim rows partners and reviewers can cite:** perfect-attendance awards (they raised absences), general after-school programs and attendance, online credit recovery, growth mindset, one-summer book mailing, student pay-for-grades, and programs for young people out of school, whose gains are equivalency credentials that do not count in the graduation rate.

## Largest overturns and downgrades

No current link was fully overturned, but four were cut back:

- **L-02 (math tutoring): strong to good.** Both Math Corps trials that used the Minnesota state test found no significant difference, and Nashville's tutoring at scale had no math effect. Plan on 0 to 0.10 on the state test.
- **L-13 (school-day social and emotional learning and tests): B to suggestive.** The best pooled estimate is 0.08 (math 0.08, English 0.07). It shrinks in large rigorous trials, and the clearinghouse finds named programs not significant. M-P19's proposed overturn was declined because the pooled effect is still positive. The federal seven-program trial did not measure test scores, so it cannot be used either way.
- **L-36 (community schools and chronic absence): B to suggestive.** Chronic absence fell in New York City and California but not in Tennessee, where it rose.
- **L-72 (summer jobs and dropout and graduation): B to suggestive.** Boston's lottery result stands, but Chicago found no change in persistence. The Summer Training and Education Program trial does not test summer jobs, because its control group also worked.

Also revised: L-77 (career academies: one Wake County lottery found graduation up 8 points, mostly for boys), L-14 (two trials that measured graduation found no direct effect), L-85, L-88, L-47 and L-79.

## 21st Century Community Learning Centers, across all indicators

- **Attendance (S1): associated only (L-44).** Matched comparisons that adjust for prior-year attendance in Texas, New Jersey and Philadelphia find slightly fewer absences for regular participants: about 0.5 to 3.4 days a year in Texas, and about three quarters of a day in New Jersey, where the comparison group is low attenders. Randomized trials of after-school programs found no attendance effect, so centers cannot earn tested credit. The state "share under 90 percent who improved" figure, such as Pennsylvania's 66 percent, reflects regression to the mean and never counts.
- **Tests (S5 to S8): near zero, not recommended (L-12).** The national randomized trial was null. Texas's median center effect was minus 0.02, and students below 45 days scored lower in math. Rhode Island reading was 0.055, Washington was 0.03 to 0.04 one year and about zero the next, and West Virginia, New Jersey 2024 and Philadelphia showed no difference. Through attendance, the gains imply at most about 0.02 in math (L-83).
- **Dropout and graduation (S3, S4): no qualifying evidence.** The only adjusted studies are two of LA's BEST, one program from one evaluator (Q-P01). The California statewide report could not be reached.
- **Science, Keystone exams, industry exam: nothing found.**

## Indicators that still have no link a partner could earn

- **S2 teacher attendance:** only district policy evidence. No staff-support program has been tested on it.
- **S9 science and S11 Keystone Biology:** the best study, Urban Advantage at 0.05, is one team's quasi-experiment. Out-of-school science programs were inconsistent.
- **S12 Keystone Literature:** policy debate in Boston (+0.13 in English, with higher graduation) is the nearest candidate but has only one strong quasi-experiment.
- **S13 industry certification exam:** one Pennsylvania cooperative-education study with an unread design. Youth CareerConnect was null.
- **S3 and S4** rest only on summer jobs (suggestive), plus the on-track and attendance mechanism links. The Quantum Opportunity Program (Q-P09) is the closest partner model, but it failed its seven-site replication. It and policy debate (PR-10) are the two to revisit.
- **S10 Keystone Algebra I** has math tutoring (good), but no study used the Keystone exam itself.

## Flags for the next step

- **Proposals resting on claims the checkers found wrong:**
  - Q-P10 and Q-P23: the sources showed positive effects, not nulls.
  - PR-07: R-033 found a small significant effect.
  - PR-04: M-045 found significant positive tutoring effects, so "consistent nulls" is overstated.
  - M-P27: M-092.
  - P-01 and P-02: P-013.
- **Proposals resting on unreachable sources:** P-27 and T-P06.
- **L-17 (belonging intervention):** another thread may hold a failed replication (G-044). It was not reviewed here.
- **Low- or no-cost levers with tested support:**
  - personalized absence messages to families (about 4 to 9 dollars per student)
  - repeated free books
  - Safe Passage-style adult presence on school routes
  - stopping perfect-attendance awards.
