## Hierarchy

| Level | Axis | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Part | Who changes | Y young people · F families and other adult participants · A staff, organizations and systems |
| Domain | Broad construct | 13 domains. The model never needs to name it: it is the code's prefix. |
| Category | Dimension of the construct | 2 to 5 per domain, taken from the anchor framework where one exists (CASEL competencies, Farrington's behaviors vs mindsets, Keyes' two continua). A category may hold one code when the framework dimension has one observable indicator. |
| Code | Observable indicator | What the model and reviewers assign. Format `Y1.1`, flat within the domain; the category is a lookup, not part of the code. |

## How to code

These rules replace today's rulesText sections where they differ. They carry forward
everything the 2.4.0 prompt already says about splitting, uncoded rows, confidence,
Subject Area and target population.

1. **Who changes decides the part, first.** Young people → Y. Caregivers, households,
   and other adult participants who are not staff (adult learners, older adults) → F.
   Staff, volunteers, the organization, or the wider system → A. The sentence's
   subject decides: "Parents will communicate better with their children" → F1.1;
   "Youth will communicate better with their parents" → Y3.4. Parenting is F even
   when the parent is a teenager. A young person's own housing, food or income is F2
   with target population `students_youth`.
2. **Output or outcome.** Program-level counts, rates and dosage ("served 300", "85%
   average daily attendance", "mentors completed 6 hours of training") → A2.5. A
   per-person threshold framed as a young person's choice ("each youth will return")
   → Y2.6, or F1.9 for families. Never mark an output uncoded.
3. **Most specific definition wins.** Choose the code whose definition, not just its
   label, names the construct. The "Use instead" lines override this rule.
4. **Split** when a statement names two or more distinct results. Do not split off the
   means, activity or setting ("by designing murals", "while leading a cleanup",
   "following staff training"). A combined indicator a source defines as one (WIOA
   "employed or in education") stays one outcome (Y7.5).
5. **Uncoded** only for headers, fragments, program names, evaluation or data notes,
   and text too vague to code. Faith language goes to the nearest construct (Y5.5
   purpose and faith growth, Y6.1 values, Y3.1 belonging, Y6.5 service).
6. **Confidence** says how well the codebook fits: high (definition or example clearly
   matches), medium (reasonable best fit), low (forced; no code names the construct),
   none (uncoded only). A practical skill with no academic content (gardening, bike
   repair) has no code: use the closest fit at low confidence. The v2 draft's residual
   "other applied skills" code is not carried over.
7. **Subject Area** must agree with a Y1 subject code: Y1.1 ELA; Y1.2 ELD; Y1.3 Mathematics;
   Y1.4 Science, Computer Science, Engineering or STEM; Y1.5 Social Studies (history,
   geography, economics) or World Languages; Y1.6 Visual & Performing Arts. Civics
   and government go to Y6.2 and health education to Y8.3, with the Subject Area still
   recorded.
8. **Social and economic mobility** (carried over from 2.4.0 rule 8, CP-07-01): code
   the concrete lever; mobility named alone → Y7.5 (youth) or F2.1 (household); social
   mobility framed as networks → Y3.5.
9. **Order carries no weight.** Domain and code numbers do not signal priority.

**Optional in phase 4: `outcome_type`.** A separate field for kind of change
(knowledge, skill, attitude, behavior, status, output; UW Extension logic model,
verified), as in the v2 draft. It lets reports cut any domain by kind of change
without adding codes. Coders agreed on it for 85% of statements in cycle 02.
Recommended, but only after the schema test below.

**Enum budget.** Keeping today's schema, the proposal's footprint is about **160**
(13 domains counted twice, 98 codes + "none", unchanged smaller enums): under the 180
test cap but past the 165 redesign line in STANDARDS S2.3. Deriving the domain from the
code prefix (PLAN.md phase 3) brings it to about **134**, or 141 with `outcome_type`.
Either way the schema must be tested live before cut-over.

## Part Y: Young people


### Y1 Academic Learning & Achievement

What young people learn and how they progress through school: demonstrated learning by subject, the behaviors and beliefs that shape learning, and school and postsecondary status. All academic outcomes live here, in three categories.

**Categories:** A. Subject learning (Y1.1–Y1.8) · B. Learning behaviors & mindsets (Y1.9–Y1.12) · C. Educational progress & attainment (Y1.13–Y1.18) · 18 codes


#### Y1-A. Subject learning


##### Y1.1 Literacy: Reading & Writing

*Short label:* Literacy · *Flag:* **Deductive** · *2.4.0 source:* 1.1; part of 9.1

- **Definition:** A demonstrated gain in reading (phonics, fluency, vocabulary, comprehension, reading level) or in writing and composition in any genre.
- **Include:** Reading level, fluency, comprehension scores; reading or ELA test proficiency; writing quality, essays, published writing presented as a writing skill.
- **Exclude:** Confidence or habits about reading or writing; oral presentation; English learners' language acquisition; pre-K pre-literacy.
- **Use instead:** Y1.12 confidence about reading aloud; Y4.6 public speaking; Y1.2 English learners; Y1.8 pre-K pre-literacy; Y2.5 creative writing as self-expression with no stated skill gain.
- **Typical example:** "Students will improve reading fluency by one grade level." (gold S048)
- **Borderline example:** "Student reporters will publish at least two articles in the community newspaper." (gold S150). Y1.1 when the program frames publishing as writing skill (the gold chose writing); Y2.5 if framed as expression or performance.
- **Theoretical basis:** ESSA 20 U.S.C. 6311(b)(1)(C) (verified); National Reading Panel (2000) (located); Common Core CCR Anchor Standards for Writing (verified).


##### Y1.2 English Language Development & Multilingualism

*Short label:* English language development · *Flag:* **Deductive** · *2.4.0 source:* 1.4

- **Definition:** Growth in English proficiency for English learners (listening, speaking, reading, writing), or maintenance and development of a home language alongside English.
- **Include:** ELP level gains, reclassification, WIDA/ELPAC-type scores; bilingual or home-language maintenance for youth.
- **Exclude:** A native English speaker learning another language (a world language); adult ESL.
- **Use instead:** Y1.5 world languages; F1.6 adult English classes; Y5.2 pride in one's language as identity.
- **Typical example:** "Youth will improve their English language proficiency." (gold S028)
- **Borderline example:** "Refugee youth will maintain fluency in their home language while learning English." (gold S143). Y1.2: language development in both languages. Y5.2 only if the statement is about pride or identity.
- **Theoretical basis:** WIDA English Language Development Standards Framework, 2020 ed. (located).


##### Y1.3 Mathematics

*Short label:* Mathematics · *Flag:* **Deductive** · *2.4.0 source:* 1.2

- **Definition:** A demonstrated gain in math skill, problem-solving or grade-level math proficiency.
- **Include:** Math test scores and proficiency; specific math skills; math used in money contexts when the outcome is the math.
- **Exclude:** Math confidence or growth mindset; grades or GPA in a math class; money-management habits.
- **Use instead:** Y1.12 math mindset; Y1.15 grades; Y7.6 budgeting and saving.
- **Typical example:** "Students will improve their math problem-solving skills and grade-level proficiency." (gold S208)
- **Borderline example:** "Students will accurately calculate percentages and interest in math class." (gold S050). Y1.3: the outcome is the calculation. "Understand the cost of credit card interest" would be Y7.6.
- **Theoretical basis:** ESSA 20 U.S.C. 6311(b)(1)(C), mathematics (verified).


##### Y1.4 Science, Technology & Engineering

*Short label:* Science, technology & engineering · *Flag:* **Deductive** · *2.4.0 source:* 1.8

- **Definition:** A demonstrated gain in science (life, physical, earth, environmental), engineering design, or computing and coding, including digital-literacy skills.
- **Include:** Science test proficiency; ecosystem or ecology knowledge; engineering design process; coding, computational thinking, digital skills.
- **Exclude:** STEM interest; STEM identity; online safety; industry certifications; advanced course enrollment.
- **Use instead:** Y2.2/Y2.3 interest; Y5.3 'a STEM person'; Y6.7 online safety; Y7.4 certifications; Y1.16 enrolling in advanced STEM courses.
- **Typical example:** "Students will apply the engineering design process to build and test a prototype." (gold S104)
- **Borderline example:** "Youth designed and built a functioning robot and presented it at the regional competition." (gold H008). Y1.4 for the engineering; the presentation is the setting, not a second outcome.
- **Theoretical basis:** ESSA 6311(b)(1)(C), science (verified); NGSS Lead States (2013), three dimensions (verified); CSTA K-12 CS Standards (2017) (verified).


##### Y1.5 Other Academic Subjects

*Short label:* Other academic subjects · *Flag:* **Deductive** · *2.4.0 source:* 1.3

- **Definition:** A demonstrated gain in history, geography, economics, world languages or another academic subject not covered by Y1.1-Y1.4 or Y1.6.
- **Include:** History and geography content; economics concepts; a world language learned by a native English speaker; other ESSA well-rounded subjects.
- **Exclude:** Civics and government (Y6.2); health education (Y8.3); personal finance (Y7.6); practical skills with no academic content.
- **Use instead:** Y6.2 civics; Y8.3 health knowledge; Y7.6 financial capability; Y1.7 research and source evaluation.
- **Typical example:** "Students will explain the causes of a major historical event." (2.4.0 codebook example)
- **Borderline example:** "Youth will explain how a bill becomes a law in their state." (gold S254). Y6.2, not Y1.5: civics and government have one home in the proposal.
- **Theoretical basis:** ESSA 20 U.S.C. 7801(52) well-rounded education (verified); NAEP 20 U.S.C. 9622(b)(2)(D) (verified).


##### Y1.6 Arts Learning

*Short label:* Arts learning · *Flag:* **Deductive** · *2.4.0 source:* 1.9

- **Definition:** A demonstrated gain in technique, vocabulary or knowledge of an art form (dance, media arts, music, theatre, visual arts).
- **Include:** Instrument proficiency; dance or art vocabulary; juried or assessed artistic skill.
- **Exclude:** Creating or performing as self-expression or experience, with no stated skill gain.
- **Use instead:** Y2.5 creative expression and performance; Y2.3 a lasting passion for an art form.
- **Typical example:** "Students will develop technical proficiency on their chosen instrument." (gold S199)
- **Borderline example:** "Youth will present their artwork in a public exhibition, building pride and confidence." (gold S077). Y2.5 (presenting as an experience), not Y1.6, because no skill gain is stated.
- **Theoretical basis:** National Core Arts Standards (2014) (verified; the processes organize the standards, cycle 04 PARTIAL on 'definition of learning').


##### Y1.7 Critical Thinking, Inquiry & Media Literacy

*Short label:* Critical thinking & media literacy · *Flag:* **Deductive** · *2.4.0 source:* 9.2

- **Definition:** Analyzing and evaluating information and sources, research and inquiry skills, and media literacy, in any subject.
- **Include:** Evaluating source credibility; identifying misinformation; research projects framed as inquiry skill; analyzing conflicting sources.
- **Exclude:** Online privacy and safety; interpersonal problem-solving; analyzing power and injustice.
- **Use instead:** Y6.7 online safety; Y4.10 personal decision-making; Y6.3 critical consciousness.
- **Typical example:** "Youth will evaluate the credibility of online news sources and identify misinformation." (gold S155)
- **Borderline example:** "Students will analyze conflicting sources about a local environmental issue and propose a policy." (illustrative, not from the gold). Split: Y1.7 for the source analysis and Y6.4 for the policy proposal if it is presented to decision-makers.
- **Theoretical basis:** NAMLE, Core Principles of Media Literacy Education (verified); Conley, Key Cognitive Strategies (located).


##### Y1.8 Early Learning & School Readiness

*Short label:* Early learning & school readiness · *Flag:* **Deductive** · *2.4.0 source:* 1.7

- **Definition:** Early language, literacy, math and cognitive development, and kindergarten readiness, for children below kindergarten age.
- **Include:** Pre-literacy (letter recognition, phonemic awareness); early numeracy; readiness assessments; broad developmental milestones on screeners such as the ASQ.
- **Exclude:** Early motor skills; developmental screenings received (a service); social-emotional skills of young children.
- **Use instead:** Y8.2 motor skills; Y8.5 screenings and referrals received; Y4 for social-emotional skills at any age.
- **Typical example:** "Children will demonstrate age-appropriate pre-literacy skills (letter recognition, phonemic awareness)." (gold S025)
- **Borderline example:** "Infants and toddlers will receive developmental screenings and referrals as needed." (gold S061). Y8.5: receiving a screening is health care access. A screening RESULT (milestones met) is Y1.8 (H064).

##### Y1.9 Academic Engagement Behaviors

*Short label:* Academic engagement behaviors · *Flag:* **Deductive** · *2.4.0 source:* 5.1

- **Include:** Homework completion rates; asking questions and taking part in class discussions; coming prepared.

- **Borderline example:** "Students will ask questions in class and participate in discussions." (gold S111). Y1.9 (a behavior in class). Y2.2 only if framed as curiosity about a topic.

##### Y1.10 Academic Perseverance

*Short label:* Academic perseverance · *Flag:* **Deductive** · *2.4.0 source:* 5.2

- **Definition:** Sticking with difficult schoolwork or learning tasks despite setbacks, including task persistence in young children.
- **Include:** Persisting on hard problems; not giving up on assignments; early-childhood task persistence.
- **Exclude:** General persistence outside learning; frustration tolerance as emotional regulation.

- **Typical example:** "Children will show persistence and curiosity when working on puzzles and new tasks." (gold S220)
- **Borderline example:** "Youth will demonstrate improved frustration tolerance during homework time, leading to more assignments completed on time." (gold S179). Split: Y4.2 (frustration tolerance) and Y1.9 (assignments completed), as the prompt split rule already requires.

##### Y1.11 Learning Strategies & Study Skills

*Short label:* Learning strategies · *Flag:* **Deductive** · *2.4.0 source:* 5.3

- **Definition:** Using specific methods to learn: note-taking, planners, test preparation, self-quizzing, metacognition.
- **Include:** Named study techniques; planning schoolwork with a tool.
- **Exclude:** General organization outside school; goal-setting in general.
- **Use instead:** Y4.3 general organization and goals.
- **Typical example:** "Students will increase their use of effective study strategies such as note-taking and test preparation." (gold S221)
- **Borderline example:** "Students will use a planner to organize their assignments." (2.4.0 codebook example). Y1.11 because a school-specific tool is named; "keep their belongings organized" would be Y4.3.

##### Y1.12 Academic Mindsets & Self-Efficacy

*Short label:* Academic mindsets · *Flag:* **Deductive** · *2.4.0 source:* 5.4

- **Definition:** Beliefs about oneself as a learner: academic self-efficacy, belonging in academic work, and growth mindset about intelligence or ability to learn.
- **Include:** 'I can succeed in this class'; believing intelligence or math ability grows with effort; confidence in academic abilities.
- **Exclude:** General self-confidence; growth mindset about challenges in general.

- **Typical example:** "Students will believe they can pass their algebra class." (gold S216)
- **Borderline example:** "Youth will adopt a growth mindset when facing setbacks." (gold S243). Y5.4: no learning context is named. S034 "intelligence can grow" is Y1.12.

- **Include:** GPA; passing core classes; credits for promotion; unspecified academic achievement.
- **Exclude:** A test score in a named subject; graduation; attendance.
- **Use instead:** The subject code (Y1.1-Y1.6) for a named-subject test; Y1.17 graduation; Y1.13 attendance.

- **Borderline example:** "Athletes will maintain passing grades to remain eligible for the team." (gold S109). Y1.15: the status is grades; eligibility is the context.

##### Y1.16 Advanced Coursework & Dual Credit

*Short label:* Advanced coursework · *Flag:* **Deductive** · *2.4.0 source:* new

- **Definition:** Enrolling in or completing advanced courses: AP, IB, honors, advanced STEM electives, dual enrollment and college credit earned in high school.
- **Include:** AP/IB/honors enrollment; dual enrollment; college credit before graduation.
- **Exclude:** Interest in a subject; postsecondary enrollment after high school.

- **Definition:** Graduating from high school, earning a GED or equivalency, or re-enrolling after leaving school.
- **Include:** On-time graduation; GED; re-engagement after dropout or release.
- **Exclude:** Adult GED (caregivers); staying enrolled through disruption.
- **Use instead:** F1.6 adult education; Y1.13 school stability.
- **Typical example:** "Youth will re-enroll in school or obtain a GED following release." (gold S241)
- **Borderline example:** "Teen mothers continued or re-enrolled in school within 6 weeks of giving birth." (gold H052). Y1.13 (the gold): mostly continued enrollment. Pure re-enrollment after leaving would be Y1.17.
- **Theoretical basis:** ESSA four-year adjusted cohort graduation rate, 20 U.S.C. 6311(c)(4)(B) (located).


##### Y1.18 Postsecondary Enrollment, Persistence & Completion

*Short label:* Postsecondary enrollment & completion · *Flag:* **Deductive** · *2.4.0 source:* 9.7

- **Definition:** Enrolling in, persisting in, or completing college or other postsecondary education or training after high school.
- **Include:** College enrollment the fall after graduation; first-to-second-year persistence; degree or certificate completion.
- **Exclude:** Applications and FAFSA (the process); college credit in high school; combined 'employed or in education' indicators.
- **Use instead:** Y7.2 applications and FAFSA; Y1.16 dual credit; Y7.5 for WIOA-style combined indicators.
- **Typical example:** "Graduates will enroll in a two- or four-year college the fall after high school." (gold S190)
- **Borderline example:** "Program graduates will be employed or in education six months after exit." (gold S033). Y7.5: a combined WIOA indicator stays one outcome (v2 rc1 rule).
- **Theoretical basis:** National Student Clearinghouse Research Center, High School Benchmarks (verified); IDEA Indicator 14 Measure A (partial).


### Y2 Engagement, Interest & Creative Expression

How young people experience learning and activities: enjoyment, curiosity, lasting interest, seeing relevance, creative expression, and choosing to take part.

**Categories:** A. Enjoyment & interest (Y2.1–Y2.4) · B. Expression & participation (Y2.5–Y2.6) · 6 codes


#### Y2-A. Enjoyment & interest


##### Y2.1 Enjoyment & Joy

*Short label:* Enjoyment · *Flag:* **Deductive** · *2.4.0 source:* 2.1

- **Definition:** A positive emotional experience of the program or of learning: fun, happiness, excitement, pride in being part of it.
- **Include:** Reports of enjoying activities; excitement to attend; pride in the program.
- **Exclude:** General life satisfaction or flourishing; feeling safe or accepted.
- **Use instead:** Y8.7 life satisfaction; Y3.1 belonging.
- **Typical example:** "Youth will report feeling excited and happy to attend program each day." (gold S234)
- **Borderline example:** "Students will say they feel proud to be part of the program." (gold S008). Y2.1 (the gold): pride in the program as an experience. "Feel they belong here" would be Y3.1.
- **Theoretical basis:** Muhammad, Five Pursuits, Joy (verified).


##### Y2.2 Curiosity & Triggered Interest

*Short label:* Curiosity · *Flag:* **Deductive** · *2.4.0 source:* 2.2

- **Definition:** Interest sparked in the moment: curiosity, asking questions about something new, willingness to try a new activity.
- **Include:** Asking questions during a demonstration; trying new activities; showing curiosity.
- **Exclude:** Interest that lasts or is pursued beyond the program; general confidence to take on challenges.
- **Use instead:** Y2.3 sustained interest; Y5.4 confidence.
- **Typical example:** "Students will ask questions and show curiosity during science demonstrations." (gold S128)
- **Borderline example:** "Campers will try an activity that initially made them nervous, such as swimming in the lake." (gold S049). Y2.2 (v2 gold): willingness to try something new. Y5.4 if framed as building confidence.
- **Theoretical basis:** Hidi & Renninger (2006), phase 1 triggered situational interest (verified).


##### Y2.3 Sustained & Individual Interest

*Short label:* Sustained interest · *Flag:* **Deductive** · *2.4.0 source:* 2.6

- **Definition:** Interest that persists, deepens or becomes the young person's own: a passion, returning to a topic, pursuing it beyond the program.
- **Include:** Discovering a passion; continuing an activity after the program; self-directed pursuit of a topic.
- **Exclude:** Momentary curiosity; interest in a career field; identity ('I am a ___ person').
- **Use instead:** Y2.2 curiosity; Y7.1 career interest; Y5.3 domain identity.
- **Typical example:** "Youth will continue pursuing robotics after the program ends, joining a school team." (gold S214)
- **Borderline example:** "70% of participants reported increased interest in pursuing a career in a STEM field." (gold H007). Y7.1: interest in a career is career exploration (v2 rc1 rule).
- **Theoretical basis:** Hidi & Renninger (2006), phases 2-4 (verified).


##### Y2.4 Relevance & Utility Value

*Short label:* Relevance & utility value · *Flag:* **Deductive** · *2.4.0 source:* 2.3

- **Definition:** Seeing learning as useful for one's goals, future plans or community.
- **Include:** Explaining how a subject connects to careers or daily life; valuing what is learned.
- **Exclude:** Career awareness; general purpose in life.
- **Use instead:** Y7.1 career exploration; Y5.5 purpose.
- **Typical example:** "Students will explain how algebra relates to careers they are interested in." (gold S115)
- **Borderline example:** "Youth will say the program helped them see why school matters for their future." (illustrative, not from the gold). Y2.4. Y5.5 only if the statement is about hopes for the future without the learning link.
- **Theoretical basis:** Wigfield & Eccles (2000), utility value: 'how a task fits into an individual's future plans' (partial; appendix A R05).


#### Y2-B. Expression & participation


##### Y2.5 Creative Expression & Performance

*Short label:* Creative expression & performance · *Flag:* **Hybrid** · *2.4.0 source:* 2.4, 2.5

- **Definition:** Self-expression through making something (art, music, writing, design), presenting or performing finished work, or responding to art, as an experience rather than a stated skill gain.
- **Include:** Composing, creating, exhibiting, performing, critiquing or connecting art to personal meaning.
- **Exclude:** A stated technique or knowledge gain in an art form; a stated writing skill gain; teamwork during a production.
- **Use instead:** Y1.6 arts skill; Y1.1 writing skill; Y4.7 collaboration.
- **Typical example:** "Youth will compose an original song." (gold S058)
- **Borderline example:** "Students will critique a peer's artwork using the language of the discipline." (gold S197). Y2.5 (the gold, NCAS Responding). "Using the language of the discipline" could argue for Y1.6; code Y1.6 only when vocabulary gain is the stated outcome.
- **Theoretical basis:** National Core Arts Standards (2014): Creating; Performing/Presenting/Producing; Responding; Connecting (verified). Hybrid: merging creating and presenting, and separating both from skill gains, is a codebook decision (CP-04-01) rather than the standards' own split.


##### Y2.6 Participation in Enriching Activities

*Short label:* Participation in activities · *Flag:* **Deductive** · *2.4.0 source:* 6.3

- **Definition:** An individual young person's choice to take part in constructive activities, inside the program (returning, joining) or outside it (sports, arts, clubs, faith groups).
- **Include:** Returning for another year; joining a team or club; participating fully alongside peers; per-person participation thresholds.
- **Exclude:** Aggregate program attendance, counts or rates; the program's accommodations.
- **Use instead:** A2.5 program-level counts; A2.4 accommodations.
- **Typical example:** "Youth will choose to return to the program for a second year." (gold S003)
- **Borderline example:** "Participants attended weekly youth group and volunteered at least once in the community this semester." (gold H080). Split: Y2.6 (participation) and Y6.5 (service).
- **Theoretical basis:** Search Institute 40 Developmental Assets, Constructive Use of Time (located).


### Y3 Belonging & Relationships

The relationships around a young person: belonging, supportive adults, friends, family, and wider networks.

**Categories:** A. Connectedness (Y3.1) · B. Close relationships (Y3.2–Y3.4) · C. Networks (Y3.5) · 5 codes

##### Y3.1 Belonging & Mattering
